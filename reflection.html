INDIVIDUAL REFLECTION

ADRIANO, JARGON YURI J.

When I read some books of Jose Rizal, it gave me a better understanding of the struggles and the experiences of Filipinos on the Spanish colonial period. Before reading his works, I mostly knew Rizal as the national hero who wrote Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo. Somehow, learning his writings made me realize that his books has deeper meanings and were meant to show the problems happening in Philippine society.

What I found interesting is how Rizal used different kinds of writing to express his ideas. In his essay named “Sobre la indolencia de los filipinos”, he explained that Filipinos were not naturally lazy, but the conditions created by the colonial rule contributed to their situation. In his work named “Filipinas Dentro de Cien Anos”, he discussed the possible future of the Philippines and how the country could eventually change.

In his work named “Sa mga kababaihang taga Malolos” also showed his belief in education, especially for women, and encouraged them to think for themselves. Meanwhile for his last work “Mi Ultimo Adios,” showed his love for his country and his willingness to sacrifice his life for it.

In the end, Rizal’s work made me understand how powerful a simple writing and knowledge can do. He used his talent not just to create stories, but to question the problems of society and inspire Filipino to become more aware of their rights and responsibilities.

Even though his work was made like about a hundred years ago, many of his ideas about education and social problems still exist today. For me, studying Rizal works is not only remembering him as a national hero, but also understanding the message he wanted to leave for the future generations.

ALIA, MARISSA D.

While studying Rizal's different works, I realized that there is more to him than just being a national hero who wrote Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo. His poems, essays, letters, and novels showed how observant he was about the problems happening around him.

What I found meaningful was that he used his writing to express what many Filipinos were experiencing and to make people think about their situation. A La Juventud Filipina also stood out to me because of its message to the youth. It made me think that even as a student, I already have the ability to contribute something, even if it is only through small things.

Another work that really caught my attention was Mi Último Adiós. Knowing that Rizal wrote it before his execution made the message of love and sacrifice for the Philippines more meaningful.

His works made me realize that knowledge is not only something we use for school or for ourselves. It can also be used to speak up, inspire others, and make a difference.

I may not be able to do the same things Rizal did, but I can apply his values in my own way by taking my studies seriously, being aware of the problems around me, and using what I learn for something good.

INDIVIDUAL REFLECTION

BARADIA, RAINIER L.

Reading Jose Rizal’s works shows how powerful books can be in changing a country. Through his writings, he bravely showed the corruption and unfair treatment Filipinos experienced under Spanish rule. His stories helped people see the real problems in society so they could start fixing them.

Through Noli Me Tangere, Rizal taught us that education and peaceful reform are needed to improve society. However, El Filibusterismo showed a serious warning about what happens when unfairness goes on for too long. It proved that constant abuse leads to anger, but true freedom cannot be won through revenge.

In his shorter writings like Makamisa and La Visión Del Fray Rodriguez, Rizal showed that people should not follow corrupt leaders blindly. He used humor and clear points to encourage everyone to think for themselves.

Meanwhile, his personal student memoirs showed that love for one's country starts with family and hard work at school.

Overall, Rizal's writings teach us that we cannot fix our problems if we pretend they do not exist. His message is still important today because it reminds us to always stand up for the truth.

CARIGMA, REISHA LOUISE L.

Jose Rizal's writings makes me realize how powerful words can be. He did not write just for fun. He used his words to help Filipinos see the truth about Spanish rule and to help them feel proud of themselves.

What I like most about Rizal is how he could write in two different ways. He could write very beautiful, sweet poems about his country, but he could also write sharp stories to attack bad rulers.

Rizal used poems to show his love for the Philippines. In his poem "A Filipinas", he describes the country as a beautiful, peaceful goddess. Back then, Spanish rulers made Filipinos feel weak and low. Rizal’s poem changed that. He reminded everyone that their homeland is beautiful and worthy of love. This teaches us that before we can protect our country, we must love it first.

Rizal was very smart and brave. When bad church leaders attacked his famous book "Noli Me Tangere" he did not just stay quiet. He wrote a story called "La Vision del Fray Rodríguez". In this story, he showed how foolish and greedy some rulers were. He used humor to fight back, proving that you do not always need weapons to show the truth.

In the end, Rizal’s writings teach us an important lesson.

We should not just follow orders without thinking. He wanted Filipinos to go to school, think clearly, and speak up against bad things. For students today, Rizal challenges us to love our history, study hard, and use our minds to make our country a better place.

CRUZ, ROGUE MARI MAGENTA B.

Reading Dr. José Rizal’s essays, poems, historical commentaries, and letters makes me realize how deeply a single individual's ideas can awaken an entire nation. Away from his famous novels, these shorter works offer a direct look into his mind, showing how he transformed personal encounters with colonial injustice, such as the execution of Gomburza and the eviction of his family in Calamba, into a lifelong mission for intellectual liberation.

Through essays like "The Indolence of the Filipinos" and his historical annotations of Morga’s Sucesos, Rizal systematically debunked the myth that Filipinos were naturally inferior, restoring a sense of pride by proving that his countrymen possessed a rich, industrious culture prior to colonization.

His progressive vision for women, most famously expressed in his letter "To the Young Women of Malolos", feels remarkably modern and deeply inspiring. In praising the young women who fought for their right to open a night school, Rizal argued that a society cannot advance if half its population is kept in forced submissiveness and ignorance.

He urged women to cultivate their intellect, think critically, and reject blind obedience, emphasizing that strong, educated mothers are essential to raising free citizens. By positioning women as active drivers of national progress rather than passive onlookers, Rizal challenged deep-seated colonial stereotypes and laid the groundwork for gender equality in the country.

Ultimately, reflecting on Rizal’s broader literary output, culminating in the poignant selflessness of his farewell poem, "Mi Último Adiós" leaves me with a profound appreciation for the power of the written word.

He proved that genuine social change does not begin on a battlefield with weapons, but in the human conscience through education and critical thought. His life and writings serve as a timeless reminder that confronting injustice, honoring our history, and championing equality are ongoing responsibilities for every generation.

ESPABILLA, JENNY C.

Rizal did not fight with weapons; he fought with words. From Noli Me Tangere to La Visión del Fray Rodríguez, he used his intelligence and courage to reveal the truth about the injustices happening in society.

Despite the criticism and danger he faced, Rizal continued to stand for what he believed was right. His writings were not only meant to tell stories but to open the eyes of Filipinos and inspire them to care for their country.

Even today, Rizal’s message remains relevant. Our country continues to face issues such as corruption, inequality, misinformation, and injustice. With so much information available to us, it is easy to know what is happening, but it is also easy to scroll past, stay silent, and pretend that these problems do not affect us.

“Kabataan ang pag-asa ng bayan, ngunit paano kung ang kabataan ay pinipiling magbulag-bulagan sa nangyayari sa bayan?”

Being called the hope of the nation is not only about achieving our personal dreams or becoming successful. It also means being aware of what is happening around us and caring about the future of our country.

Rizal’s life reminds us that sometimes, the greatest danger is not speaking up, but choosing to remain silent when we know something is wrong.

We may not all be like Rizal, and we may not have the power to change everything overnight. But we can start by opening our eyes, seeking the truth, and choosing to care.

The youth cannot be the hope of the nation if we continue to close our eyes to the struggles of the nation.

FENEQUITO, MARIA EDELYN C.

After learning about the different works of José Rizal, I realized that his writings were not only made because he was a good writer, but because he wanted to express what he saw and felt about the Philippines and its people.

From Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo to his poems, essays, and other writings, I noticed that many of his works focused on education, freedom, injustice, nationalism, and the importance of thinking for ourselves.

What stood out to me is that Rizal used different ways to express his ideas. Some of his works were serious and political, while others were more personal and focused on youth, love for the country, and his own experiences.

This made me understand that Rizal was not just a national hero who wrote two famous novels. He was also a student, writer, thinker, and someone who used his knowledge to speak about the problems of his time.

As a student, I find his works meaningful because they remind me that education should not only be about passing subjects. It can also help us understand our society and become more aware of what is happening around us.

Rizal’s works made me appreciate why his writings are still studied today and why his ideas continue to be relevant to Filipinos.

INDIVIDUAL REFLECTION

NACHURA, ALEXA L.

Reading the different writings and poems of Jose Rizal made me realize that his works were not only written to show his talent, but also to express his love for the Philippines and his hopes for the Filipino people. Each of his works has its own message, and most of them focus on important issues such as freedom, education, injustice, and the future of the country.

In Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo, Rizal showed the problems Filipinos experienced during Spanish rule, such as unfair treatment, abuse of power, and inequality. Mi Último Adiós showed his deep love for the Philippines and his willingness to sacrifice himself for his country.

His works A La Juventud Filipina and To the Young Women of Malolos highlighted the importance of education and the role of the youth in improving society.

His poems, such as Mi Retiro, Himno a Talisay, Me Piden Versos, and A Filipinas, showed his love for nature, homesickness, and appreciation for his homeland. Junto al Pasig and El Consejo de los Dioses showed his creativity and ideas about society and the Philippines.

His unfinished works, Makamisa and Memorias de un Estudiante de Manila, also showed his desire to portray Filipino life, while La Visión del Fray Rodriguez reflected his thoughts about society and religion.

Overall, Rizal's works made me realize that writing can be a powerful way to express one's feelings and fight for change. He did not use weapons to fight for his country; instead, he used his knowledge, ideas, and writings.

His works encouraged Filipinos to become educated, think for themselves, and love their country. For me, the most important lesson from Rizal is that even a single person can make a difference when they use their talents and knowledge for the greater good.

His writings are still meaningful today because they remind us to value education, stand against injustice, help others, and be responsible citizens of our country.
